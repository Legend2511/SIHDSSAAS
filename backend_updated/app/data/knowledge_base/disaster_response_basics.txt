# Disaster Response Basics

## Flood Preparedness and Response
- Move to higher ground immediately if flood waters are rising
- Do not walk or drive through moving flood water — even 15 cm of moving water can knock you down
- Store drinking water in clean containers as flood water contaminates supply
- Keep important documents in waterproof bags at an elevated location
- Disconnect electrical appliances if flooding is imminent
- Avoid contact with flood water — it may contain sewage, chemicals, or sharp debris
- After flooding, do not consume food that has been in contact with flood water
- Boil drinking water until authorities confirm supply is safe
- Watch for snakes and insects that may have been displaced by flooding
- Do not return home until authorities declare the area safe

## Cyclone Preparedness and Response
- Board up windows and secure doors before cyclone arrival
- Store at least 3 days of water (4 liters per person per day) and non-perishable food
- Charge all mobile devices and keep a battery-powered radio
- Identify the strongest room in your house — interior rooms without windows are safest
- Stay indoors during the cyclone, away from windows and external walls
- The eye of the cyclone brings a temporary calm — do not go outside, the second half will arrive
- After the cyclone, watch for downed power lines and damaged structures
- Report gas leaks immediately and avoid using open flames
- Fishermen should not venture into the sea until the all-clear is given by authorities
- Coastal communities should move at least 2-5 km inland or to an elevation above the expected storm surge

## Heatwave Precautions
- Stay hydrated — drink water regularly even if not thirsty
- Avoid outdoor work during peak hours (12 PM to 4 PM)
- Wear lightweight, light-colored, loose-fitting clothing
- Never leave children, elderly, or pets in parked vehicles
- Use ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution) or homemade salt-sugar water
- Watch for heat exhaustion signs: heavy sweating, weakness, nausea, dizziness
- Heat stroke is life-threatening: hot dry skin, confusion, unconsciousness — call emergency services immediately
- Farmers should schedule field work for early morning or late evening
- Keep livestock in shade and provide extra water
- Sprinkle water on rooftops and surroundings to reduce ambient temperature

## Earthquake Safety
- DROP, COVER, and HOLD ON during shaking
- If indoors, stay inside — move away from windows, heavy furniture, and shelves
- If outdoors, move to an open area away from buildings, trees, and power lines
- If driving, pull over safely and stay in the vehicle
- After shaking stops, check for injuries and damage before moving
- Be prepared for aftershocks which can occur minutes to months later
- Do not use elevators after an earthquake
- Check gas, water, and electrical lines for damage
- Keep an emergency kit with water, food, first aid, flashlight, and important documents

## Landslide Awareness
- Watch for warning signs: unusual sounds, new cracks in walls or ground, tilting trees, muddy water in streams
- If you suspect a landslide, evacuate immediately and alert neighbors
- Move to higher ground perpendicular to the expected path of the slide
- Stay away from the slide area even after it appears to have stopped — further sliding is possible
- Avoid river valleys and low-lying areas during heavy rainfall in hilly terrain
- Plant vegetation on slopes to reduce erosion risk
- Do not build on steep slopes, near drainage channels, or on natural erosion areas
